Almost every tumbler and lidded mug uses stainless steel. The catch is that **there are many types of stainless**, and not all of them are equally good for drinks. Here are the differences you will run into most often.

## Summary

| Grade | Main content | Rust resistance | For drink containers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 304 (18/8) | About 18% chromium, 8% nickel | Good | Food-grade standard, most common |
| 316 | Like 304 + about 2% molybdenum | Very good | More resistant to acids and salt, more expensive |
| 201 | Low nickel, replaced with manganese | Lower | Not recommended for the inner wall |
| 430 | No nickel | Moderate | Usually for the outer body, not the inside |

## Stainless 304

This is the most common grade for tumblers and kitchenware. **18/8** means roughly 18% chromium and 8% nickel. Chromium forms a protective layer against rust, while nickel makes it more stable and easier to shape.

**Good for:** water, coffee, tea, and everyday drinks.

## Stainless 316

Stainless 316 adds **molybdenum**, making it more resistant to salt and acids. That is why it is also called *marine grade*.

**Good for:** people who often fill their tumbler with acidic drinks like orange juice, or who live near the coast. It costs more than 304.

## Stainless 201

Stainless 201 reduces nickel and replaces it with manganese to lower the cost. As a result, it **rusts more easily**, especially with acidic drinks or harsh cleaning chemicals.

**Note:** not recommended for the inner wall of a tumbler that touches your drink.

## Stainless 430

This grade contains no nickel and is magnetic. Its rust resistance is below 304. In tumblers, 430 is sometimes used for the **outer body**, while the inside is still 304.

## The magnet test myth

Many people test stainless with a magnet: if it does not stick, it must be 304. **This is not reliable.** Stainless 304 can become slightly magnetic after it is formed, and 201 may not stick either. The safest approach is to **ask the seller about the inner material**, or look for a stamp such as "SUS 304" on the bottom of the product.

## Single wall vs double wall

- **Single wall:** one layer of metal. Light and cheap, but it does not hold temperature. Hot drinks make the outside hot too.
- **Double wall vacuum:** two layers of metal with a vacuum in between. Drinks stay hot or cold longer, and the outside does not get hot or sweat.

## Outer coating and engraving

A tumbler's color and pattern (such as wood grain or matte colors) come from the **outer coating**, not the metal. Engraving etches through this coating to reveal the metal underneath. That is why engraving looks crisp and does not peel.

## How to choose

1. **Ask about the inner material.** The part that touches your drink matters most.
2. **For hot or cold drinks, choose double wall.**
3. **Care for it properly.** Avoid bleach and steel wool. Read our [care guide for tumblers and mugs](/en/blog/tumbler-bamboo-care-guide/).
